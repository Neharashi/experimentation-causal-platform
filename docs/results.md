## 2. Peeking: false-positive rate and power

20,000 users per simulated test, 300 simulations per scenario, α = 5%.

| Procedure | False-positive rate (no effect) | Power (+15% lift) |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-horizon test, single look at the end | 3.7% | 93.0% |
| Naive p-value checked at 20 looks | **29.3%** | 95.7% |
| mSPRT always-valid p-value, 20 looks | 1.0% | 70.0% |
| O'Brien-Fleming, 5 looks | 5.7% | 92.0% |

Peeking with a naive p-value inflates false positives several-fold. Both sequential procedures keep them near the 5% target; mSPRT buys unlimited looks with some power, O'Brien-Fleming keeps nearly full power with planned looks.

When mSPRT stopped on a real effect it used on average 12,138 users instead of 20,000.

## 4. Causal effect recovery (simulated confounding, true ATE = 2.0, 20 seeds)

| Estimator | Mean estimate | Bias | RMSE | 95% CI coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive difference | 3.55 | +1.55 | 1.55 | – |
| Propensity score matching (DoWhy) | 2.09 | +0.09 | 0.15 | 100% |
| LinearDML (EconML) | 1.99 | -0.01 | 0.04 | 100% |

Matching CIs come from a bootstrap, which is known to be conservative for nearest-neighbour matching (Abadie & Imbens, 2008).

Difference-in-differences (TWFE, true effect 5.0): mean estimate 5.07, bias +0.07, CI coverage 85%.

## 5. Real datasets

**LaLonde (NSW treated vs CPS controls)** — experimental benchmark ATT ≈ $1,794.

- Naive difference: $-8,498
- Propensity score matching ATT: $1,707 (95% CI $2 to $3,193)
- LinearDML ATT: $1,243

**California Proposition 99** (cigarette packs per capita, treatment from 1989)

- Synthetic control ATT 1989-2000: -19.5 packs (-24%), pre-period RMSPE 1.66, placebo p = 0.077
- Naive DiD (TWFE): -27.3, but pre-trend test p = 1e-49 — parallel trends fails, which is exactly why synthetic control is the right tool here.
